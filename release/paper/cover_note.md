## Summary for a cover letter (150 words)

This article measures how public attention to notable people rises and decays after their deaths, using monthly English Wikipedia pageviews for 16,397 people who died in 2016 to 2023, drawn from a fixed hash sample of Wikidata. Attention is normalised by total Wikipedia traffic and by each person's own pre-death level, and the analysis follows a plan written before outcomes were computed. The median peak is 16.5 times the pre-death level; decay is best described by a fast phase (half-life about 11 days) and a slow phase (about 6.9 months), although a power law predicts later cohorts slightly better. Two to three years on, the median person is back to 0.98 of their earlier attention. People who die young keep far more attention, a difference that a placebo date shows is not a pre-existing trend; occupational differences partly are. Data and code are openly released.
